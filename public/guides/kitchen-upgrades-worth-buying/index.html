Kitchen

Kitchen upgrades worth buying for the way you cook

Make a small kitchen feel more intentional with useful countertop pieces and storage that respects your space.

A Linen & Form buying guide · 3 min read

A styled kitchen can still be a working kitchen. The key is to improve the things you already touch every day, rather than covering the countertop with decorative accessories. Think about your three most common meals and where preparation tends to get inconvenient.

The considered approach

Keep the work area clear. Choose upgrades that make preparation, washing, or storage easier.

The pieces to consider

Start with the category that solves a real problem in your space. These are selection guides, not tested product recommendations.

01
A category to consider

A practical everyday cutting board

For a preparation surface that also looks at home on the counter

A sufficiently sized board gives you a defined place to prepare ingredients. When not in use, its material can add texture to a plain kitchen. Choose by stability, working area, washing practicality, and care routine before deciding on the finish.

Before you buy: Confirm dimensions, food-use suitability, care instructions, and whether it fits your sink and storage.

When to skip it: Skip an oversized decorative board you cannot comfortably clean.

A category to consider

A simple utensil holder

For tools used so often that a drawer is inconvenient

Keeping a small number of frequently used tools together can reduce rummaging during cooking. A holder is most useful when it does not become storage for every utensil you own. Choose enough weight and width for the tools you plan to keep in it.

Before you buy: Check base stability, internal opening, cleaning instructions, and distance from heat sources.

When to skip it: Skip if your countertop is already short on preparation room.

A category to consider

A compact dish-drying solution

For a sink area that never feels quite clear

A drying rack or mat should match the dishes you wash and where water will go. A smaller, well-planned setup can be easier to live with than a large rack left half empty. Think about cleaning the rack itself too.

Before you buy: Measure the available area and compare drainage, cleaning access, and open-cabinet clearance.

When to skip it: Skip if it obstructs the tap or leaves water where it cannot drain appropriately.

Protect the preparation space

Identify the area where you chop, mix, or plate food and keep that space available. A beautiful canister set is less helpful if it forces you to prepare dinner on a tiny corner. Store rarely used appliances away from the main work area when practical.

Choose a small number of visible finishes that relate to your existing kitchen. A wood board, simple ceramic utensil holder, and coordinated towel can add warmth without a renovation. They still need to be easy to clean and appropriate for how you use them.

Buy for washing and storage too

Measure the sink, drying area, and cabinet space before selecting bulky accessories. A board may fit the counter but be awkward to wash. A rack may hold enough dishes but prevent a cabinet door from opening. Check the whole routine, not one moment of it.

For anything used with food, verify intended use and care instructions from the manufacturer. Do not assume that every decorative vessel is suitable for food storage or that every container is microwave or dishwasher safe. Prefer clearly described specifications over an attractive image alone.

Your final check before checkout

Measure the space, verify the exact item’s specifications, and compare the complete price and return terms. Use what you already own if it does the job. A considered room comes together through fit and function, not the number of purchases.
