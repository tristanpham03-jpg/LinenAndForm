Lighting

Affordable lighting with a high-end look

Choose lamps by shade, scale, and placement for a room that feels softer after sunset.

A Linen & Form buying guide · 3 min read

The appearance of a lamp is only half the decision. Think about the light it produces, the objects it reflects in, and the way you will turn it on. A room often benefits from well-placed lamps more than from adding another decorative object.

The considered approach

Plan where the light should go first. Then choose a fixture that suits the scale of the space.

The pieces to consider

Start with the category that solves a real problem in your space. These are selection guides, not tested product recommendations.

A category to consider

A fabric-shade table lamp

For a sideboard or bedside that needs a softer visual presence

A simple shade can balance a sculptural base and reduce the visual prominence of an exposed bulb. Its fabric and shape influence the appearance of the light. Choose it while considering both daytime styling and evening use.

Before you buy: Check shade width, bulb requirements, switch placement, cable length, and manufacturer safety information.

When to skip it: Skip if the shade crowds the surface or blocks access to essentials.

A focused reading floor lamp

For a chair that needs its own light

A dedicated lamp can make an underused corner more practical. A directional head lets you consider where the light lands, while the base determines how much floor space it needs. Test the position relative to the chair before choosing a style.

Before you buy: Compare reach, adjustability, base stability, cord route, and suitable bulb specifications.

When to skip it: Skip if the base would obstruct the walkway beside the chair.

A plug-in wall light

For a small bedside with limited table space

Moving a light off the table can leave more room for daily essentials. A plug-in design still needs a safe mounting method and a suitable outlet. The cord is part of the visible arrangement, so plan it rather than treating it as an afterthought.

Before you buy: Verify installation requirements, wall compatibility, cord length, switch access, and rental permissions.

When to skip it: Skip if you cannot install it according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Create useful layers of light

Notice what you need to do in the room: read, talk, prepare food, or move through it. A table lamp may suit an evening corner but not provide enough useful light for a task elsewhere. Place light around the activities rather than buying several lamps and finding homes for them later.

Compare the lamp’s height and shade with the furniture beside it. Check sightlines from a seated position so a bright bulb does not sit directly in view. Reflective screens and mirrors can change how comfortable a placement feels.

Read the electrical details

Confirm the fixture’s bulb type, maximum rating, dimmer compatibility, and power requirements. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions for installation and use. A decorative finish says nothing about electrical suitability, so choose listings with clear specifications and appropriate safety information for your location.

Plan the cord route without creating a trip hazard or trapping a cord under furniture. For a rental, check your lease before drilling or changing fixtures. If a fixture requires electrical work, use a qualified professional rather than treating it as a decorative styling task.

Your final check before checkout

Measure the space, verify the exact item’s specifications, and compare the complete price and return terms. Use what you already own if it does the job. A considered room comes together through fit and function, not the number of purchases.
